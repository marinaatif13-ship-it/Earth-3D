Introduction to Earth

Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only place in the universe known to host life. Its surface is a living mosaic of oceans, continents, ice and cloud, shaped over billions of years.

A thin atmosphere, liquid water and a magnetic field work together to keep the temperature stable and shield every living thing from harsh radiation.

Earth is the third planet from the Sun

The only known planet to support life

70% of the surface is covered in water

Light from the Sun takes about eight minutes to arrive. It warms the oceans, drives the weather and powers nearly every living system on the planet.

Fact of Earth

Tectonic Plates
and Movements

Earth's crust is divided into plates

Seven major plates and many smaller ones float on the semi-molten mantle, drifting a few centimetres every year — about as fast as fingernails grow.

Causes earthquakes, mountains & volcanoes

Where plates collide, slide past or pull apart, pressure builds and releases. That is how the Himalayas rose, how the Pacific “Ring of Fire” formed, and why the ground shakes.

Earth and Space

Rotation
24 hours (causes day & night)
Revolution
365 days (causes seasons)

Earth spins on a tilted axis while it circles the Sun. That tilt, not distance, is why we have seasons — and the Moon’s pull gives us the tides.

Climate Change
and Pollution

Burning fossil fuels, clearing forests and dumping waste are changing the balance of air, land and water faster than natural systems can adapt.

Global warming

Rising temperatures melt ice and raise sea levels.

Air and water pollution

Smoke, plastics and chemicals harm health and habitats.

Deforestation

Fewer trees means less oxygen and less stored carbon.

Conservation & Sustainability

Reduce

Use less energy, water and packaging.

Recycle

Turn old materials into new products.

Reuse

Repair, share and give things a second life.

Summary of Key Points

Earth is a small, living planet: a molten core, a moving crust, a thin protective atmosphere and a water cycle that connects every ocean, cloud and river.

Its balance is fragile. Cutting pollution, protecting ecosystems and using resources wisely keeps this home habitable for everyone who comes next.
